---

## The rule that keeps this small

There are far fewer frames than cards. A whole set — sometimes a whole block of sets —
shares one frame; only the artwork and the printed data change. So a blank is needed per
**block × energy × stage**, not per card.

Energy here is *our* mapping (`tcgEnergyForGen`), because that is what picks the frame:
Bug→grass, Ice→water, Ghost→psychic, Rock/Ground→fighting, Normal/Flying/Dragon→colorless,
and **Poison→grass for Gen 1–2 but psychic for Gen 3–4**. Steel and Dark fall back to
colorless until Metal/Darkness frames exist.

Stage follows the TCG baby rule: Pichu → Pikachu stays Basic, Raichu is Stage 1.

## About 1st Editions

The "Edition 1" stamp only ever existed in the WotC era — **Base Set through the Neo
block**. So it matters for the Gen 1 and Gen 2 lists below: use **Unlimited** prints
(no black stamp left of the artwork). Shadowless is fine too.

From the EX era onward — all of Gen 3 and Gen 4 — no 1st Edition prints exist, so
there is nothing to avoid.

# How to hand them over

Same as the HGSS set. Drop the finished PNGs in
`~/Projects/personal/pokedex/HGSS_Card_Templates/` (or a sibling folder per block — say
`PL_Card_Templates/`) named so the block, energy and stage are obvious, e.g.
`pl_water_stage1.png`.

Don't worry about size, the art hole, or alignment — I do all of that:

1. Resize to 1062×1480 and punch the transparent art hole up to the rail
2. Measure the per-energy art window and the text anchors for the block
3. Wire per-block routing so each card uses the frame of its own source set
4. Bump the cache version, test, and ship

**Leave the artwork window filled in** — I punch it out. What I need gone is the printed
TCG data: attack names, costs and damage, the HP number, the Poké-BODY / Poké-POWER box,
the flavour text, the NO./HT/WT bar under the art, weakness/resistance/retreat *values*
(keep the labels), the illustrator credit and the set number. Keep the frame, the borders,
the stage tab, the pre-evolution circle, the type symbol and the set symbol.
